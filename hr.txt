Norvik Logistics HR Policy v6.0, effective 2026-01-01. Every employee is assigned a band. Band A covers operational staff such as warehouse operators and drivers. Band B covers administrative and junior professional roles. Band C covers senior professionals and team leads. Band D covers managers and principal specialists. Band E covers directors and members of the executive committee. Contractors and interns are not assigned a band and follow the terms of their individual contract or internship agreement. The band determines notice periods, training budget, probation length, eligibility for a corporate card and several expense limits, as described in the Expenses Policy.

The standard working week is 40 hours in Barcelona and Madrid and 39 hours in Lisbon, according to local agreements. Office staff work flexible hours with mandatory core hours from 10:00 to 16:00 CET. In July and August, an intensive schedule applies on Fridays, which end at 15:00. Warehouse staff in Band A work in shifts defined by the operations calendar and are excluded from flexible hours and from the summer Friday schedule. Overtime is only paid to Bands A and B and must be approved in advance by the manager; Bands C to E are compensated for extra hours with time off, up to 5 days per year.

Remote work is allowed up to 2 days per week once the probation period is completed. Probation lasts 3 months for Bands A and B and 6 months for Bands C, D and E. Warehouse roles are not eligible for remote work. Full remote work requires the written approval of the relevant Vice President and is reviewed every 12 months. Employees may work from another country for up to 20 working days per calendar year, provided they notify HR in advance and also complete the IT travel notification required for connections from outside the European Union. Working abroad beyond 20 days requires prior approval from HR and Legal because of tax implications.

Employees are entitled to 23 working days of vacation per calendar year in Spain. One additional day is granted for every complete 5 years of seniority, up to a maximum of 26 days. Lisbon employees are entitled to 22 working days under local law, plus the same seniority days, with the same maximum of 26. Up to 5 unused days may be carried over to the following year, but they must be taken before 31 March or they are lost. Employees who join during the year receive a pro-rated number of days, rounded up to the nearest half day. Vacation requests of more than 10 consecutive working days must be submitted at least 30 days in advance.

Sick leave must be reported to the manager before the start of the working day. A medical certificate is required from the third consecutive day of absence. The company tops up the statutory sick pay to 100 percent of the base salary for the first 30 days of each absence; afterwards, only the statutory amount applies. Parental leave follows local law in each country. In addition, the company grants 2 extra paid weeks of parental leave to every parent, regardless of gender, which must be taken within the first 12 months after the birth or adoption.

Employees who are eligible for remote work receive a one-time home office allowance of 300 euros once their probation period ends, to buy a chair, desk or other furniture. The receipts are submitted to HR, not through the expense app. In addition, remote-eligible employees receive a monthly internet stipend of 25 euros, paid automatically through payroll. Because the stipend is paid through payroll, home internet costs must never be claimed as business expenses. Employees in full remote work receive a one-time allowance of 600 euros instead of 300, but the internet stipend remains the same.

Each employee has an annual training budget: 1,200 euros for Bands A to C and 2,000 euros for Bands D and E. Unused budget does not carry over to the next year. Courses are requested through the HR portal and need the manager's approval. Any professional certification costing more than 800 euros includes a 12-month retention clause: if the employee resigns within 12 months of completing the certification, they must repay the cost pro-rated by the remaining months. Conference attendance is paid from the training budget, but the related travel and hotel costs are business expenses under the Expenses Policy.

Performance reviews take place twice a year, in March and in September. Promotions decided in a review cycle take effect on the first day of the following month, so a promotion approved in September takes effect on 1 October. A new band applies immediately from that date for salary, notice period, training budget and expense limits. However, the IT equipment entitlement linked to the new band only applies at the next scheduled laptop refresh. The probation period is not repeated after a promotion. Mid-cycle promotions are possible only with approval from the HR Director and take effect on the first day of the month after approval.

Disciplinary measures are proportionate to the severity of the conduct. A first minor breach leads to a verbal warning, documented by the manager. A second minor breach within 12 months, or a first serious breach, leads to a written warning. Serious breaches include fraudulent expense claims, sharing credentials, and losing a second company device within 24 months, as described in the IT Policy. Very serious breaches, such as harassment or theft, can lead to dismissal. Employees always have the right to be heard before a written warning is issued and may be accompanied by a workers' representative.

Notice periods for resignation are 15 days for Bands A and B, 1 month for Band C and 2 months for Bands D and E. All company equipment, including laptops, phones, access cards and corporate cards, must be returned within 5 business days after the last working day; unreturned items are deducted from the final payment at their residual value. Unused vacation days are paid in the final settlement. Training retention clauses still apply, so a certification repayment may also be deducted. Exit interviews are optional. Where this policy and a collective agreement differ, the more favourable condition for the employee applies.
